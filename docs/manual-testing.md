# Manual testing with real children

Automatic tests cover the logic; only a child can tell you whether something is clear, fun and not too hard. Test with children of the
right age, one at a time, with a parent nearby but quiet. Do not help unless the child is stuck for more than 30 seconds, and write down
**what you did to help**: that is the finding.

Sit beside the child, hold nothing, and look at the child's face, not the screen. Use a real phone (not the emulator) with the sound on.
Keep each session to 10 minutes. Always end on something the child can do.

## Before you start

- A phone with the latest build and an internet connection for the first minutes (the Explorers units download once, then work offline).
- A new child profile with the real age (the track follows from the age and the skills ticked).
- Pens and the sheet at the end of this file (or the same columns in a note).

## Little Learners (ages 3-5)

- Opens straight to the unit map; taps a unit; plays a letter lesson; every game starts with Dandoona's voice and the hand showing what to do.
- Questions: does the child know where to tap without help? Do they understand the star and the next-unit lock? Do they ask what the hand means?

## Explorers (ages 6-8)

Use a child of 6, 7 or 8 who is **not** told what the games are. For each activity below the question is the same:
**does the child understand it from the demo alone (the hand and Dandoona's voice), without you saying anything?**

### 1. The start (parent and child)
- [ ] The parent can answer "What can your child already do?" without asking you what a line means.
- [ ] The summary says the track and the starting unit; the parent agrees with both.
- [ ] A 6-year-old with no skills starts at Letters inside the Explorers map and does not feel "babyish": watch for sighs, "this is for babies".

### 2. Each new activity

| Activity | What the child should understand | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Sound Tap | tap each box to hear its sound, then read the word | taps the whole word instead of the boxes; does not link the sound to the letters |
| Word Builder | hear the word, tap or drag the letters in order | the drag (does the finger slip?); a wrong letter: does the shake make sense? |
| Spell It | the same, with the picture hidden until the word is built | frustration when no picture; does the picture appearing feel like a reward? |
| Read & Pick | read the word, tap the picture | reads out loud or guesses from the pictures; a/an and plurals ("a cat", "cats") |
| Find the Word | the word is said, find it among three cards | confuses look-alike words (was/saw, the/then) |
| Sentence Builder | put the cards in order to make the sentence | does the sentence mark (. ! ?) confuse; does the child read it aloud when done |
| Fill the Gap | pick is/are, a/an, has/have, can | does the picture (one or many) help or mislead |
| True or False | does the sentence match the picture | the cross and the check: which is "yes" for the child? do they read, or look at the picture only? |
| Sight Word Hunt | the word is said; tap its bubble among floating bubbles | bubbles move too fast or too slow; does a wrong bubble discourage |
| Listen & Tap, Match, Memory | as in Little Learners, with Explorers words | too easy? the child gets bored in two minutes |

For each: [ ] understood from the demo alone  [ ] needed the "?" button  [ ] needed an adult  [ ] too easy  [ ] too hard  [ ] fun (smiles, "again!").

### 3. Instructions and sounds
- [ ] Is Dandoona's instruction clear and short enough? Does the child wait for it to finish, or tap over it?
- [ ] The letter sounds (b, c, d… short a, e, i, o, u; the digraphs sh, ch, th; the vowel teams ay, ee, ie, oa, oo): does the child say the same sound back? Write down any that confuse them (the owner's audio review page lists them, `content/generated/listen-check.html`).
- [ ] Words said wrongly or oddly ("car", "kite", "van", "hat" were flagged): does the child repeat them correctly?

### 4. Too hard? Too long?
- [ ] After 10 minutes, does the child ask to continue? (a good sign)
- [ ] Which unit made the child stop? (Digraphs and Blends are the first steep ones.)
- [ ] Do stars come too easily or never? A child who gets 1 star every time is stuck, not learning.
- [ ] The map: does the child understand where to go next? Do they notice the review and the treasure chest?

### 5. Rewards
- [ ] The chest, the new outfit for Dandoona, the story, the certificate: does the child want them? Do they ask to open the wardrobe?
- [ ] The active-days badge: is it a happy thing or a pressure ("I missed a day")?

### 6. The parent
- [ ] Can the parent find the child's progress in each track and switch the active track (with the confirmation)?
- [ ] "Seems to know this already. Skip ahead?" card (only if "I'm not sure" was ticked): is it clear, not annoying?
- [ ] Forgot password: the code arrives by e-mail within a minute; the new password works.

## ملخّص بالعربي
- اجلس بجانب الطفل ولا تساعده إلا إذا علق أكثر من 30 ثانية، واكتب **كيف ساعدته**: هذا هو الاكتشاف.
- لكل لعبة جديدة في المستكشفين: هل فهمها الطفل من العرض التوضيحي (اليد وصوت دندونة) وحده؟
- راقب: هل بدا له المحتوى سهلًا جدًا أو صعبًا، وأي وحدة جعلته يتوقف، وأي صوت أو كلمة نُطقت بشكل غريب.
- في الأخير: هل يطلب أن يكمل؟ إن قال نعم فاللعبة ناجحة.
